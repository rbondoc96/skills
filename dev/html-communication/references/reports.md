# Reports

Use when the reader needs to assess findings or make a decision from evidence.

1. Lead with the finding and its scope. Separate observed results, proposals, and unresolved questions with explicit text labels.
2. Order the main reading path around the decision: finding, supporting evidence, implications, and next action. Use only the sections needed by this report.
3. Place definitions and supporting observations beside the evidence they explain. Keep limitations that change the conclusion with the conclusion itself.
4. Choose a table for direct comparisons, key-value pairs for one record, and ordered steps for a process. Preserve units, denominators, conditions, and source dates where they affect interpretation.
5. Put raw logs and lengthy supporting data in labeled disclosure sections or appendices. Keep decisive evidence visible without expansion. Add navigable sources that the intended reader can access.
6. Recalculate summary counts from source records, identifying the counted unit and population. Check every claim against evidence, including captions and footnotes added during editing. If the report is regenerated, edit its sources or generator rather than its output. Missing required inputs must be reported, not silently omitted.

Use the [component catalog](components.md) for the reusable layout and markup. Read the [explainer branch](explainers.md) when readers must understand a mechanism before they can judge the findings.

Done when a reader can identify the conclusion, its evidence and limits, and the decision or action it supports without reading raw logs.
