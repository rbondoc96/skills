# Stakeholders

Use when the reader needs to decide, prioritize, fund, or coordinate work. Apply the shared foundation in `SKILL.md`; use this reference to select decision-relevant substance.

## Make the decision or status clear

- For a decision, state the choice needed and a recommendation when the evidence supports one. Explain the consequences of the meaningful alternatives, including deferral when relevant.
- For a status update, state the outcome so far, material blockers, and next action. Identify the responsible person or team and timing only when known.
- Connect technical facts to effects on users, delivery, cost, reliability, or obligations when those effects are supported. Do not manufacture business value for every technical change.
- Separate estimates and targets from commitments. State assumptions and uncertainty that could change the decision, cost, or schedule.
- Include enough evidence to assess the recommendation. Use implementation detail when it explains a material constraint or tradeoff.

## Match responsibility and expertise

Determine which decision or coordination task belongs to this reader. A stakeholder may be deeply technical; adapt to demonstrated knowledge rather than removing all technical terms.

If no decision or action is required, say what changed without inventing an ask. Do not force an executive-summary template onto a short update.
