# Writing for humans

Make the reader's job easy. State the point, provide enough context to understand it, and preserve everything they need to act correctly.

Use this foundation for replies, drafts, and edits. All guidance is bundled here; no other skills or particular project files are required.

## 1. Choose the audience guidance

Identify what the reader needs to understand or do and what they already know. Follow explicit audience instructions first, then infer from the conversation and supplied material. Ask only when missing information would materially change the output.

Read only the reference needed for the current writing task:

| Reader's task | Guidance |
|---|---|
| General communication, or no evidence that specialist guidance is needed | This file alone |
| Understand, build, debug, maintain, or review a software system | [Developers](references/developers.md) |
| Decide, prioritize, fund, or coordinate work | [Stakeholders](references/stakeholders.md) |
| Serve readers with different knowledge or responsibilities | [Mixed audiences](references/mixed-audiences.md) |

A technical topic alone does not establish a developer audience. A job title does not establish subject knowledge. A developer deciding whether to fund a migration may need stakeholder guidance.

For mixed audiences, read the mixed-audience reference first. Add a specialist reference only when a section needs that depth. The shared purpose determines emphasis; do not combine every reference by default. Reconsider the route when the audience or purpose changes.

## 2. Write the substance

- Lead with the answer, outcome, or request, then explain the context and causal steps needed to understand it. If an explanation failed, restore missing context before simplifying its words.
- Name who does what and what happens as a result. Prefer active voice when the actor matters, familiar words, and concrete verbs.
- Keep useful technical terms, briefly explaining unfamiliar ones. Use established terminology consistently. Preserve exact identifiers, commands, and quotations where accuracy depends on them.
- Preserve facts, numbers, units, conditions, exceptions, uncertainty, and consequences. Keep qualifications beside the claims they limit. Distinguish possibilities, recommendations, commitments, and observed results.
- Add specifics only when supported. Do not invent mechanisms, deadlines, measurements, or experiences. Preserve material ambiguity explicitly or ask for clarification.
- Give each paragraph one main idea. Split sentences that require backtracking. Use examples or analogies when they clarify; explain an analogy's limits when they affect the conclusion.

This approach is informed by ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, especially consistent terminology and clear sentence structure. It does not require its controlled dictionary or establish compliance with the standard.

## 3. Edit the voice and form

- Match the requested tone and preserve useful parts of the author's voice. Use grammatical sentences, natural contractions, and sentence lengths that fit the thought.
- Remove stock openings, inflated claims, ceremonial transitions, vague abstractions, forced personality, and conclusions that merely repeat the answer. State points directly without unnecessary rhetorical contrasts.
- Avoid em dashes. Use a period, comma, or rewritten sentence. Judge other wording in context instead of treating individual words as proof of machine authorship.
- Follow an explicit format request or required template. Otherwise, infer the simplest useful form. Audience and format are separate; neither needs a required argument.
- Use paragraphs for connected explanations, lists for parallel points, and numbered steps when order matters. Add headings only when they aid navigation. Keep prerequisites and warnings with the affected instruction.
- Provide the requested depth. Shorten wording without cutting necessary context or reasoning; brevity is not a fixed word limit.

For before-and-after illustrations of evidence, terminology, and unsupported judgments, read [editing examples](references/examples.md) when those distinctions are difficult to apply.

## 4. Check and deliver

Verify that the reader can find the point immediately, follow the reasoning, and act without missing a material condition. Check added facts against the source and confirm that the selected audience, terminology, tone, and format fit the request.

Return requested drafts or rewrites without a preamble or editing report unless requested. Resolve material missing information before presenting a draft as ready to use. In conversation, answer directly with the supporting context needed.

Finish when these checks pass. Stop trimming when further cuts would remove useful meaning.
